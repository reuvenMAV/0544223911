# Vocabulary curriculum policy

## Source

Use `coca-5000.csv` as the American-English frequency backbone. It contains 5,000 ranked lemma/part-of-speech entries derived from the Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA) top-5,000 list.

Use high-value word families, prefixes, and suffixes as occasional memory supports, while tracking what the learner can actually understand and produce. A known base word does not prove knowledge of every derived form, and a familiar affix does not make every constructed form valid English.

Columns:

- `Rank`: frequency position
- `Word`: lemma
- `Part of speech`: COCA tag
- `Frequency`: corpus count
- `Dispersion`: how broadly the item appears across the corpus

Common tags include `n` noun, `v` verb, `j` adjective, `r` adverb, `p` pronoun, `i` preposition, `c` conjunction, `d` determiner, `m` number, `t` infinitive marker, and `x` negation.

Source attribution: Corpus of Contemporary American English frequency list, distributed as a free top-5,000 list at https://www.wordfrequency.info/f5.asp.

## Curriculum bands

Use rank bands as rough priority pools, not CEFR equivalences:

- Core A: ranks 1–500
- Core B: ranks 501–1,000
- Everyday expansion: ranks 1,001–2,000
- Independent-use expansion: ranks 2,001–3,000
- Broad general vocabulary: ranks 3,001–4,000
- Advanced general extension: ranks 4,001–5,000

For Pre-A1 and A1, prefer concrete, useful content words from the first 1,000 plus words demanded by the learner's interests. Do not explicitly teach articles, pronouns, and grammatical function words as isolated vocabulary unless the learner's error shows a need.

For A2 and above, sample from progressively wider bands but continue filling high-frequency gaps first. A frequency rank never proves CEFR level or mastery.

## Selection procedure

Before a lesson:

1. Select due review items from `learner-progress.md`.
2. Select the active grammar or communication objective.
3. Search `coca-5000.csv` for 2–5 useful candidate lemmas or parts of speech.
4. Prefer candidates that occur naturally in the learner's selected context.
5. Reject items that are too abstract, age-inappropriate, redundant, or unlikely to be reused.
6. Teach collocations or short phrases when that is how the word is naturally used.

Do not introduce more than 3–5 new target items in a normal short lesson. A young beginner may need only 1–3.

## Evidence and recycling

Track rank, part of speech, meaning, meaningful exposures, supported uses, independent uses, failed recalls, contexts, and next review.

Use at least 5–6 meaningful encounters across more than one lesson. Require independent retrieval across at least two lessons before marking an item acquired. When the learner struggles:

1. reduce the task to recognition in a clear personal context
2. model the word in a useful phrase
3. offer a supported choice or sentence starter
4. request one original use
5. revisit later in a different interest-based context
6. test delayed retrieval without announcing the review

Do not repeat the same sentence. Vary speaker, setting, tense, question type, and interest context only when natural.

## Learner overrides

Let the learner request easier or harder words, add a domain, pause a topic, or replace interests at any time. Keep the frequency backbone, but prioritize the learner's real communicative needs over raw rank.
